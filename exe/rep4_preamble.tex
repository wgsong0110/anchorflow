\section*{Protocol}
\textbf{A (L2 tracking).} Each representation follows a reference point trajectory by minimizing the per-point L2 distance to it on its own geometry; the experiment measures how closely each representation can reproduce the reference motion, so the only reported quantity is the L2 error.
Four scenes:
(i) wolf and bread (PhysGaussian scenes): all official-opacity-filtered 3DGS Gaussian centers (wolf 139{,}018, bread 53{,}606), no interior fill, no subsampling; reference = a single Gaussian velocity field swapped every 10 frames ($\Delta t = 0.5\sigma^2/A$), 120 frames;
(ii) chair (paste) and __S2NAME__ (new shapes, NeRF-synthetic objects): 3DGS trained with the official 3DGS defaults (30k iterations, white background), reference = the per-frame particle positions of a PhysGaussian MPM simulation of the object (all simulated particles, i.e.\ the Gaussians and the PhysGaussian interior fill, no subsampling), 50 frames, normalized like (i) (frame-0 bounding box to $[0,1]^3$, longest axis 1).
chair (paste): plasticine, $E=2{\times}10^5$, $\nu=0.3$, $\rho=1000$, yield stress $200$, sticky floor, 0.3 m drop, $n_\text{grid}=100$ (i-PhysGaussian runner; 38.9\% of the 16 initial nearest neighbors separate beyond $3\times$ their initial distance, i.e.\ a permanent topology change). __S2DESC__
Each representation minimizes the L2 objective with $\Delta=-(G+\varepsilon\lambda_{\max}I)^{-1}\nabla L$, $G$ recomputed every step and an Armijo backtracking line search. $G$: Ours (hex) -- Gauss-point determinant log barrier; PhysTwin -- spring elastic energy (stiffness multiplier $k$); VR-GS (XPBD tetrahedral cage) -- tetrahedron-determinant log barrier; Spring-Gaus -- spring elastic energy.
$\varepsilon$ and $\eta$ of every method were chosen once on wolf (lowest mean RMSE over the first 3 frames) and are used unchanged for all scenes.

\textbf{Ours (hex).} Quadratic B-spline hexahedral grid, the same grid and cell-size rule as the real-time solver
(cube cell edge $=$ cell $\times$ scene MPM $dx$, lattice anchored at the simulation origin, cube edge 0.02 sim units = PG $ /100$, cell 1),
re-laid on the previous positions every frame; the unknowns are the node positions and particles follow the
B-spline-weighted interpolation $y=\sum_i w_i(X)\,x_i$, $F=I+\sum_i u_i\otimes\nabla w_i$. The metric is the
log barrier of $\det F$ at the $2{\times}2{\times}2$ Gauss points of every occupied cell (in place of the cell-determinant
log barrier); everything else (shapes, frames, field swaps, optimizer, metrics) is unchanged. Ours (hex) $\varepsilon=1e-3$, $\eta=0.5$ (chosen by the same rule as the other methods: lowest mean RMSE over the first 3 wolf frames). Code commit ff365f0.

\textbf{VR-GS.} The cage and Gaussian binding are those of our own VR-GS implementation (code is not public): the occupancy grid of the Gaussian centers (dilated by one cell) split into Kuhn tetrahedra, smallest cell with at most 30K vertices (the paper's upper bound); a Gaussian sits at the barycentric interpolation of its enclosing tetrahedron and $F=D_sD_m^{-1}$ of that tetrahedron. Fixed topology, the unknowns are the cage vertex positions ($\det F$ cumulative from rest). $G$: tetrahedron-determinant log barrier (the same form as the old tet row, weight $1/\sqrt{\#\text{tets}}$), the same form as the determinant log barrier of Ours (hex), so only the representation changes; the XPBD strain-energy (fixed corotated) metric has no inversion barrier and was not used. $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, $\eta=1$ (same rule: lowest mean RMSE over the first 3 wolf frames, 20 combinations). Code commit 8942d80 (exe/rep\_track\_vrgs.py).

\textbf{Spring-Gaus.} Official code (Colmar-zlicheng/Spring-Gaus, commit 62a1bb5) and its initial (pre-training) settings from config/mpm\_synthetic/default.yaml, DYNAMIC: 2048 mass points (the Gaussian centers voxelized at 0.01, then 2048 drawn at random with seed 123), 256 spring neighbors per mass point with rest lengths $l_0$ and stiffness $K_{ij}\propto k_j/l_{0,ij}$ ($k_j$ = soft vector, 16 fixed springs then decaying), and each Gaussian bound to its 16 nearest mass points with inverse-square-root-distance weights, $y=X+\sum_k c_k(X)u_k$ (the official interpolation of the velocity stage; the dynamic-stage absolute form does not reproduce the rest positions). Unknowns are the mass-point positions, $G$ is the spring elastic energy $\tfrac12\sum K_{ij}(|x_i-x_j|-l_{0,ij})^2$ (stiffness scale is irrelevant because $\varepsilon\lambda_{\max}$ normalizes it); the official reconstruction training (train.py) is not used because the protocol follows a kinematic target. $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, $\eta=100$ (same rule, 20 combinations on wolf). Code commit 47e462b (exe/rep\_track\_sg.py).

\textbf{Metric.} RMSE over all tracked points, averaged over frames and divided by the fixed rest bounding-box diagonal $L$.
